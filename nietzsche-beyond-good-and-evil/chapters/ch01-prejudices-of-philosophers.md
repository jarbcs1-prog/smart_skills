# Chapter 1: Prejudices of Philosophers

## Key concepts

- **Will to Truth as a problem.** The will to truth is itself a will — not a given. Ask: why truth rather than untruth, uncertainty, ignorance? The value of truth is the real question.
- **Origin of the will to truth.** Philosophers have assumed truth originates from a pure, disinterested drive. Nietzsche suspects it originates from error, deception, selfishness — genesis from opposites is the rule, not the exception.
- **Perspectivism.** Life requires perspective; "pure spirit" and "the Good in itself" (Plato) are inversions of truth. Denying perspective is denying life.
- **Dogmatism as caricature.** All great things first wander as caricatures (Vedanta, Platonism). Dogmatic philosophy was a necessary but dangerous stage; Europe's struggle against it forged a magnificent tension of soul.
- **Christianity as Platonism for the people.** The ecclesiastical oppression of millennia is the popular form of Platonic idealism.
- **Philosophers as advocates.** Philosophers are not neutral inquirers; they are advocates of their own type, defending their own physiology and interests under the guise of universal truth.

## Operational heuristics

- When a thinker claims objectivity, ask: what type of person needs this conclusion? What would threaten them if it were false?
- Treat "the Good", "the True", "the Rational" as perspectival, not absolute.
- Genesis from opposites: truth from error, generosity from selfishness, vision from covetousness.
