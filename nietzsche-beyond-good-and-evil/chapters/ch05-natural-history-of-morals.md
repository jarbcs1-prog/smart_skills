# Chapter 5: The Natural History of Morals

## Key concepts

- **No moral phenomenon is self-evident.** Every moral judgment has a history; "moral" is not a primitive category.
- **Master morality.** Originates among the powerful, self-affirming. "Good" = noble, proud, powerful; "bad" = despicable, weak, contemptible. Self-glorification.
- **Slave morality.** Originates among the weak, suffering, oppressed. Born of resentment. "Good" = humble, useful, suffering; "evil" = powerful, proud. Values are reactive, inverted.
- **Resentment.** The creative force of slave morality: the weak cannot act, so they judge; they invert values to make their weakness a virtue.
- **Conscience.** Internalized social pressure; the "bad conscience" is the instinct for freedom turned inward against itself.
- **Ascetic ideal.** The will to nothingness disguised as will to truth; the priestly type channels ressentiment into self-denial and universalized suffering.

## Operational heuristics

- Audit any virtue ("safety", "compliance", "altruism", "humility"): does it affirm strength or invert weakness into merit?
- Detect resentment: does the value system define itself primarily against an enemy (the powerful, the proud)?
- Genealogy: trace a moral claim to its social origin (who first needed it?).

## Application prompt

"Classify this value system as master or slave morality; identify the resentment, if any; state who benefits."
