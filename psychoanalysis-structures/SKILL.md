# Psychoanalytic Structures (Jung)

# Evidence & verification (prerequisite)

Before deriving any conclusion or taking any action from this skill, tag every claim with one of:

- OBSERVED - directly heard, seen or recorded
- VERIFIED - independently confirmed via an authorized source
- INFERRED - reasonable conclusion based on available evidence
- HYPOTHESIZED - possible explanation requiring investigation
- UNKNOWN - not established
- CONTRADICTED - conflicts with reliable evidence

Definitions and worked examples: `../evidence-classification.md`.
Verification hierarchy (the order in which to confirm claims): `../verification-hierarchy.md`.
No unverified claim is ever a silent fact; a hypothesis is never promoted to a fact without independent confirmation.

## Core Philosophy
Jungian structures describe psychological dynamics within an interaction — projection, role manipulation, attempts to establish dependency or authority and the agent's own assumptions and reactions. They are analytical lenses, not diagnostic labels. They must never be used to diagnose the people an agent interacts with (callers or callees).

## Prohibition
**NEVER** use Jungian analysis to diagnose, label or pathologize real callers or callees in a live interaction — the agent must not conclude "this person has a complex," "this person is projecting," or "this person is acting out an archetype" as a statement about the other party's condition. These concepts may be used to (a) understand the psychological dynamics of the interaction, (b) recognize how the agent's own assumptions are being activated and (c) identify attempts to establish dependency or authority. They must not be used as a verdict on a real person.

## Core Formula
> **Diagnostic Value = (complex identified + archetype named + projection spotted) - integration path**
A diagnosis without an integration path is a label, not a tool.

## Thinking Patterns (Mindset)
- **Before judging a reaction, ask: "Is this proportionate or is a complex constellated?"** Disproportion signals unconscious activation. But never conclude the other person has a "complex" — use this only to understand the interaction dynamics and the agent's own responses.
- **Before disliking someone intensely, ask: "What in them mirrors my shadow?"** Strong aversion is often projection.
- **Before calling someone 'difficult', ask: "Which archetype is dominating — theirs or mine?"**
- **Before prescribing a fix, ask: "Where has libido withdrawn and what repressed content is there?"**
- **Before assuming persona = self, ask: "What is the shadow compensating for?"**

## Decision Tree
```
Is the problem a disproportionate emotional reaction?
├── Yes - complex constellated - identify the complex; trace the trigger
└── No - proceed

Is the problem a strong aversion to a person/group?
├── Yes - shadow projection scan - what trait is denied in self?
└── No - proceed

Is the problem a creative/narrative block?
├── Yes - archetype identification - which archetype is stalled/missing?
└── No - proceed

Is the problem organizational/team dysfunction?
├── Yes - individuation assessment - what is being split off vs. integrated?
└── No - apply general diagnostic
```

## Procedures (Domain-Specific)
1. Step 1: Identify the presenting problem (reaction, aversion, block, dysfunction).
2. Step 2: Check proportion (disproportion -> complex constellated).
3. Step 3: Run the shadow projection scan (strong aversion -> denied trait).
4. Step 4: Identify the dominant archetype.
5. Step 5: Assess libido flow (flowing/withdrawn/blocked).
6. Step 6: Prescribe an integration path.
Workflow: problem -> proportion -> shadow scan -> archetype -> libido -> integration.

## Core Reasoning Models
1. **Libido as general psychic energy.** Not merely sexual; energy of the whole psyche. Where it flows = meaning; where withdrawn = emptiness/symptoms.
2. **Complexes.** Autonomous, emotionally charged idea-clusters in the personal unconscious; can possess the ego. Constellation = activation by a matching stimulus.
3. **Archetypes (collective unconscious).** Universal inherited patterns (Shadow, Persona, Anima/Animus, Self, Hero, Mother, Trickster). Forms, not images.
4. **Shadow.** Repressed inferior side; contains vital energy. Integration, not elimination. What we deny in ourselves, we project.
5. **Persona.** Social mask. Over-identification - hollow life; shadow compensates.
6. **Anima/Animus.** Contrasexual image within; source of mood and relational projection.
7. **Individuation.** Lifelong integration of conscious + unconscious toward the Self. Wholeness, not perfection.
8. **Active imagination.** Dialogue with unconscious figures; turns fantasy into integration tool.
9. **Projection/transference.** Unconscious contents attributed to others; recognizing it is diagnostic gold.

## Edge Cases & Failure Modes
- **Over-pathologizing:** not every strong feeling is a complex; check proportion first.
- **Shadow inflation:** identifying "my shadow" can become an excuse; integration requires action, not just naming.
- **Archetype stereotyping:** archetypes are dynamic forms, not fixed labels; avoid reducing a person to "a Hero".
- **Cultural bias:** archetypal expressions vary by culture; don't impose one culture's imagery.
## Procedures (Domain-Specific)

1. **Establish context:** client's presenting problem (reaction, aversion, block, dysfunction).
2. **Identify the problem:** name the presenting symptom or dysfunction.
3. **Check proportion:** is the reaction proportionate to the trigger? If disproportionate, a complex may be constellated.
4. **Run the shadow projection scan:** strong aversion → denied trait. Is the client denying a trait they project onto others?
5. **Identify the dominant archetype:** Shadow, Persona, Anima/Animus, Self, Hero, Mother, Trickster, etc.
6. **Assess libido flow:** flowing, withdrawn or blocked? Where did energy withdraw?
7. **Prescribe an integration path:** what would integration look like? Small, concrete steps toward wholeness.
Workflow: problem → proportion → shadow scan → archetype → libido → integration.

## Edge Cases & Failure Modes (cont.)

- **Projection vs. perception:** not every perception of another's trait is a projection; check the evidence.
- **Complex activation:** complexes activate in response to matching stimuli; a single strong reaction may be significant.
- **Transference in therapy:** the client may project feelings onto the agent; recognize and manage this.
- **Historical context:** Jung's work reflects early 20th century culture; apply it with cultural awareness.
- **Integration ≠ perfection:** individuation is lifelong; don't expect completion in a single interaction.
- **Persona collapse:** when persona fails, shadow erupts; expect regression before integration.

## Thinking Patterns (Mindset)

- **Before naming an archetype, ask:** "Is this proportionate to the trigger?"
- **Before identifying a complex, ask:** "Is there a strong aversion or block?"
- **Before projecting, ask:** "What trait is being denied and projected onto whom?"
- **Before integrating, ask:** "What would integration look like as a concrete next step?"
- **Before labeling a person, ask:** "Am I reducing them to a single archetype?"
- **Before acting, ask:** "Have I checked the libido flow status?"
- **Before concluding, ask:** "Is this a cultural artifact or a universal pattern?"
- **Before acting, ask:** "Have I checked the proportion of the reaction?"
- **Before concluding, ask:** "Is this a healthy role transition or a pathological collapse?"
- **Before acting, ask:** "Have I checked the shadow content?"
## Anti-Patterns (NEVER)
- **NEVER reduce Jung to "archetypes as stereotypes"** because they are dynamic, living patterns filled by personal experience; treating them as labels kills the diagnostic value.
- **NEVER treat the shadow as purely evil** because it contains vital, repressed energy; integration, not elimination, is the goal; eliminating the shadow eliminates life force.
- **NEVER confuse persona with the whole self** because over-identification with the mask produces hollow inauthenticity; the shadow compensates and erupts.
- **NEVER diagnose without an integration path** because a label without a path is a judgment, not a diagnosis; always end with "what would integration look like?"
- **NEVER repress instead of integrate** because repression returns as symptoms, dreams, slips; the unconscious always speaks.
- **NEVER ignore the reference point (libido flow)** because where energy has withdrawn, look for the repressed content; emptiness is a signal, not a void.
