# Sales & Negotiation

## Core Philosophy
Sales is **value exchange, not persuasion**. The skill's value is not explaining what sales is (the agent knows) but **diagnosing the deal stage, selecting the right methodology and prescribing the next move with a specific script**.

## Core Formula
> **Sales Value = (stage diagnosed) + (methodology matched) + (objection handled with value) + (next step with deadline)**
A deal without a next step and deadline is not a deal; it's hope.

## Thinking Patterns (Mindset)
- **Before pitching, ask: "Is this prospect qualified?"** Selling to the unqualified wastes everyone's time.
- **Before discounting, ask: "What am I getting in return?"** Discounting without value exchange erodes margin and teaches the buyer to haggle.
- **Before responding to an objection, ask: "Is this a real objection or a request for more information?"** Most objections are the latter.
- **Before closing, ask: "What is the next step and its deadline?"** No next step = no deal.
- **Before following up, ask: "What value am I adding, not just checking in?"** "Just checking in" is noise.

## Decision Tree
```
Is the prospect qualified (controllable/liquid/interested/decision-maker)?
├── Yes - proceed
└── No - disqualify gracefully; don't sell

What is the deal stage?
├── Discovery - SPIN questions; active listening; uncover pain
├── Qualification - CLID; up-front contract (Sandler)
├── Presentation - Challenger: teach, tailor, take control
├── Objection - acknowledge, clarify, respond with value, confirm
├── Negotiation - anchor with quote; handle "do better?" with value; time-bound close
└── Close - ask for business; trial close; confirm next step + deadline

Is price the sticking point?
├── Yes - value justification first; discount only with value in return
└── No - find the real objection (usually risk/trust/timing)
```

## Procedures (Domain-Specific)
1. Step 1: Qualify the prospect (controllable/liquid/interested/decision-maker).
2. Step 2: Build rapport and establish an up-front contract.
3. Step 3: Run discovery (SPIN questions).
4. Step 4: Present value (Challenger: teach, tailor, take control).
5. Step 5: Handle objections (acknowledge, clarify, respond, confirm).
6. Step 6: Negotiate and close (anchor, value, time-bound offer, next step + deadline).
Workflow: qualify -> rapport/contract -> discovery -> present -> objections -> close/follow-up.

## Core Mental Models
1. **Rapport first.** People buy from people they like and trust. Genuine connection before pitching.
2. **Qualify fit.** Need, budget, decision-maker, timeline. Don't sell to the unqualified.
3. **Methodologies.** SPIN (Situation/Problem/Implication/Need-payoff), Sandler (pain funnel, up-front contracts), Challenger (teach/tailor/take control). Match to context.
4. **Value over price.** Justify price through value; handle price objections with value, not discounts.
5. **Objection handling.** Objections are requests for more information. Acknowledge, clarify, respond, confirm.
6. **Negotiation.** Anchor with quote; handle "can you do better?" with value; close with time-bound offers.
7. **Follow-up.** Scheduled, templated check-ins after quotes and silence — highest-ROI lever.
8. **Ethics.** Honesty, disclosure, avoid manipulation. Long-term reputation > short-term win.

## Edge Cases & Failure Modes
- **Silent prospect:** follow up on a schedule (day 1 value-add, day 3 question, day 5 direct ask); silence ≠ no.
- **Multiple stakeholders:** map the buying committee; identify the economic buyer vs. the user.
- **Procurement/legal involvement:** route to specialist; don't improvise on contracts.
- **Unrealistic timeline:** reset expectations early; better to lose a deal than overpromise.
- **Competitor comparison:** don't bash competitors; differentiate on value and fit.
- **No decision-maker access:** qualify out or find a champion; selling to non-decision-makers wastes cycles.

## Anti-Patterns (NEVER)
- **NEVER sell to the unqualified** because it wastes both parties' time and erodes trust; disqualify gracefully and early.
- **NEVER discount to close** because discounting without value exchange erodes margin and teaches the buyer to haggle; if you must discount, get something in return (term, volume, reference, case study).
- **NEVER argue with an objection** because objections are requests for more information; acknowledge, clarify, respond, confirm.
- **NEVER skip the follow-up** because most deals are won in follow-up, not the first call; silence is not rejection.
- **NEVER bash competitors** because it signals insecurity; differentiate on value and fit instead.
- **NEVER overpromise to win** because unrealistic commitments destroy trust and reputation; under-promise, over-deliver.
- **NEVER manipulate** because short-term wins from manipulation produce long-term reputation damage and churn.
