# The Art of War (Sun Tzu)

## Core Philosophy
Sun Tzu is not a collection of tricks; it is **strategic philosophy centered on winning without fighting**. The skill's value is not quoting aphorisms but **diagnosing the strategic position and prescribing the move that avoids costly conflict**.

## Core Formula
> **Strategic Value = (position assessed) + (asymmetry found) + (cheapest winning move identified)**
Strategy that requires battle when positioning alone would suffice, is a failure.

## Thinking Patterns (Mindset)
- **Before engaging, ask: "Can we win without fighting?"** If yes, do that; battle is the last resort.
- **Before committing, ask: "What are the five factors for both sides?"** Moral Law, Heaven, Earth, Commander, Method.
- **Before revealing strength, ask: "What does the opponent believe about us?"** Deception shapes their decision.
- **Before choosing ground, ask: "Whose terrain favors whom?"** Fight only on favorable ground.
- **Before prolonging, ask: "What is the cost of time?"** Economy of force; win quickly.

## Procedures (Domain-Specific)
1. **Five-factor scoring:** list Moral Law, Heaven, Earth, Commander, Method for both sides; compare; identify the largest gap.
2. **Terrain mapping:** mark favorable/unfavorable zones; choose entry point; avoid unfavorable ground.
3. **Deception check:** state what opponent believes about your strength; state what you want them to believe; design the gap.
4. **Momentum assessment:** is shi building or stalling? If stalling, change approach, not just effort.
5. **Economy calculation:** estimate cost of 30/60/90-day prolonged conflict vs. decisive action; decide threshold.
6. **Intelligence gap closure:** list unknowns; assign intelligence-gathering actions before committing.

## Decision Tree
```
Is direct conflict unavoidable?
├── Yes - proceed
└── No - win without fighting: disrupt plans/alliances, position, deter

Can we choose the terrain?
├── Yes - select favorable ground; force opponent into bad ground
└── No - proceed with caution; consider withdrawal

Is the opponent predictable?
├── Yes - exploit the pattern; be unpredictable ourselves
└── No - gather intelligence before acting

Is the cost of prolonged conflict acceptable?
├── Yes - proceed, but set a time limit
└── No - seek quick decisive action or disengage
```

## Core Reasoning Models
1. **Winning without fighting.** Supreme art: subdue without battle via positioning, deterrence, psychological advantage.
2. **Know yourself and enemy.** Victory from understanding both sides' strengths, weaknesses, intentions.
3. **Terrain & positioning.** Ground determines advantage; choose favorable ground; force enemy into bad ground.
4. **Deception.** "All warfare is based on deception." Appear weak when strong, strong when weak; be unpredictable.
5. **Timing & momentum.** Act when conditions favor; don't fight uphill; momentum carries victory.
6. **Economy of force.** Prolonged war drains resources; win quickly; avoid sieges and attrition.
7. **Adaptability.** No fixed formations; respond to enemy's shape; water takes the vessel's shape.
8. **Intelligence.** Spies and foreknowledge essential; decisions without intelligence are guesses.

## Edge Cases & Failure Modes
- **No named opponent:** the adversary is the status quo or inertia; score against "do nothing."
- **Over-deception:** credibility is also an asset; deception that is discovered destroys trust permanently.
- **Terrain not choosable:** in digital/abstract domains, "terrain" maps to platform, timing or information advantage.
- **Adaptability vs. consistency:** being too adaptable reads as having no strategy; balance responsiveness with a clear objective.
- **Intelligence gaps:** acting without foreknowledge is gambling; invest in intelligence before committing.

## Anti-Patterns (NEVER)
- **NEVER treat Sun Tzu as a collection of tricks** because it is strategic philosophy; tricks without philosophy are manipulation, not strategy.
- **NEVER overuse deception** because credibility is a strategic asset; once deception is discovered, trust is permanently lost.
- **NEVER ignore economy of force** because prolonged conflict drains resources; a strategy that wins slowly may lose by attrition.
- **NEVER fight on unfavorable terrain** because if you cannot choose ground, withdraw or delay; fighting uphill is a choice to lose.
- **NEVER proceed without intelligence** because decisions without foreknowledge are guesses; invest in knowing before acting.
- **NEVER confuse adaptability with aimlessness** because responsiveness without a clear objective is drift, not strategy.
- **NEVER engage without an exit condition** because every engagement needs a defined point at which you withdraw; without it, sunk cost takes over and you fight to the last.
- **NEVER underestimate a weaker opponent's desperation** because a cornered adversary is the most dangerous; a weaker opponent with nothing to lose fights harder than a strong one with something to protect.
- **NEVER ignore the moral law (alignment)** because if the people are not aligned with the leader, no amount of strategy compensates; misalignment is the silent killer of otherwise sound plans.
- **NEVER treat intelligence as optional** because acting on assumption instead of foreknowledge is gambling, not strategy; the cost of intelligence is always less than the cost of a wrong engagement.
