# Technique 03 — Revival & Enrichment

**Best for:** re-engaging dormant leads, previous customers, win-back sequences and filling in missing CRM data.

**When NOT to use:** someone with no prior relationship (use `technique-01`),
or a call whose purpose is a genuine survey (use `technique-04`).

**Source:** standard outbound practice. Structured here to match 01/02 so it
loads consistently from `technique-index.md`.

---

## Core Philosophy

**Prior history is the opening.** You don't have to earn attention from cold —
you have to acknowledge the relationship and check whether anything has changed.

The mistake in revival calls is treating them as cold calls with a warmer
opener. They aren't. The recipient already has a story with you and the only
useful question is whether that story has moved.

---

## Part 1: Revival/Win-Back

### The Opener

```
"Hi [Name], it's [Name] from [Entity]. We spoke about [specific thing] back in
[month]. Did I catch you at a decent time?"
```

**Why the specificity matters.** "We spoke a while back" is weak. "We spoke
about your expansion plans in March" is credible and demonstrates you actually
know who they are.

**The three responses to the opener:**

| Response | Meaning | Move |
|----------|---------|------|
| "Oh yes, I remember" | Warm | Straight to "what's changed?" |
| "I'm not sure I remember" | Partial | Don't insist — "No worries, we spoke briefly about [X]" |
| "I don't think we've spoken" | No history | Verify you're not calling the wrong person, then exit politely |

**Never claim history you don't have.** If the record doesn't support the
reference, don't manufacture it. Being wrong here ends the call and, worse, is
the kind of error that gets reported as a scam call.

### The Has-Anything-Changed Question

This is the spine of the whole call:

```
"What's changed on your end since we last talked?"
```

Almost always the answer is yes — something got delayed, expensive, urgent, or
someone left. The call exists to surface what it is.

**Listen for four change types:**

| Signal | Example | Move |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Trigger** | "We just got a deadline", "lease renewed" | Urgency is real — qualify it |
| **Blocker cleared** | "Budget finally approved" | Convert to a concrete next step |
| **New pain** | "Actually now we're worried about X" | Re-qualify from scratch, use `technique-01` |
| **No change** | "Still on the backburner" | Offer long-term suppression, exit cleanly |

### Presenting What Changed Since Last Time

The close of a revival call is *what's different now* — not what you said last
time. This is the whole reason to call again.

```
"Two things have changed since we spoke: [change 1] and [change 2].
Based on that, the thing that'd make the most sense now is [offer]."
```

### Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "We've gone in a different direction" | "Totally understand. What direction?" — then either re-qualify or exit |
| "I'm still not ready" | "No pressure at all. Would [timeframe] be better or should I just check back rarely?" |
| "I thought I told you to stop calling" | Apologize, suppress immediately, no further pitch |
| "Things are tough right now" | Don't sell. Acknowledge, offer to check back, exit |

The hardship response is the one most agents get wrong. "Things are tough" is not
an objection to overcome; it's information that should end the sales part of the
call.

## Part 2: Enrichment (data capture)

A separate purpose that deserves its own structure, because the failure mode is
specific: **an enrichment call turns into a pitch**.

### Why This Is Its Own Flow

You have the recipient's attention and a legitimate reason for it. That's
exactly the moment an agent drifts into selling — and the recipient, who
consented to 30 seconds of questions, feels misled. You also don't yet know
enough to pitch intelligently, which is the other reason it's a mistake.

**The rule:** collect the data, stop, sell on a later call with real context.

### The Opener

```
"Hi [Name], [Entity] here. We're updating our records and you're missing
[field]. Takes about 30 seconds — okay?"
```

### Questions

Short, specific, one at a time. Three to five maximum.

| Field | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Company size | "Roughly how many people are on your team?" |
| Budget range | "What range were you thinking of?" — accept bands, don't push |
| Timeline | "Are you looking at this this quarter or further out?" |
| Decision maker | "Is this something you'd decide on or is there someone else involved?" |
| Current solution | "Are you working with anyone currently on this?" |

Budget deserves care: if they give a number, don't immediately counter. Just
record it. Countering turns data collection into a negotiation and skews the
data you collect for everyone else.

### Handling "Why do you need this?"

Answer in one sentence and move on. Being cagey about data collection is a
reason to distrust the whole call.

```
"Just so we route you to the right person and don't send you things that aren't
relevant. Two more questions?"
```

### Close

```
"That's everything — really appreciate it. Thanks [Name]."
```

Short, grateful, done. No offer, no "while you're on".

## Recording the Outcome

Enrichment and revival calls only pay off if the results are captured
structurally. At minimum, per call:

- **Revival:** change type, new timeline, disqualification reason, next action date
- **Enrichment:** each captured field, confidence and explicit refusals

Capture refusals as carefully as captures. "Won't give budget" is itself
routable data — it tells you something about how a call with them will go.

---

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Recipient doesn't remember you | Weak or fake history reference | Be specific or don't claim history |
| Drifts into pitching | Enrichment treated as a sales call | Hard gate: no offer in Flow D |
| Bad-tempered response | Revival with no value to offer | Lead with what changed, not with "just following up" |
| Wasted call | Wrong channel — they don't buy by phone | Respect channel preference, ask instead |
| Pushed into a re-qualification | New pain discovered mid-call | That's fine — switch to `technique-01` deliberately |
